There are thousands of videos on youtube on DIY retaining walls. They range from hacks stacking bricks using hope as mortar to Phds with math on whiteboards showing precisely how fucking stupid you’d have to be to make a retaining wall by stacking bricks.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-1" href="#footnote-1" target="_self">1</a></p><p>As far as I can tell, it depends. What do you need your retaining wall to retain? What do you want it to look like? How comfortable you are being around when (not if) it fails. If it keeps your pansies from spilling onto the sidewalk, stack bricks! If it keeps your house from sliding into the sea, call an engineer.</p><p>Deciding where you need to be between stack-o-bricks and Hoover Dam is the hard part. Under-engineer and you’ll regret it (because it’ll fail too soon / too catastrophically). Over-engineer and you’ll also regret it (mostly your wallet but also your back if you’re building it yourself).</p><p><em>The good news is either way you know you will probably regret it.</em></p><p>For me there are two broad paths to regret: </p><ol><li><p>Concrete footings (correct, recommended by engineers—over-engineered, basically all the houses in my neighborhood have some version of this somewhere)</p></li><li><p>Rebar footings hammered into the earth (incorrect, not recommended by engineers—under-engineered, I’ve never seen this in a residential setting but I have I’m going with the (incorrect, inadvisably engineered) rebar retaining wall method. Instead of just driving some rebar with a hammer and putting a board behind it like they do in the parks, I wanted it to look nice. Also, I wanted it to be taller than I was going to be able to accomplish with a single row of 2x12” redwood board.</p><p>The plan: 4x4s stacked elegantly with holes for the rebar to hide them. The perfect compromise between utility, cost, and aesthetics.
