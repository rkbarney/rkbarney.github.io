title: Past the Lost Coast
date: 2025-09-11

<p>Eureka sits just north of California’s Lost Coast, the only undeveloped wilderness of the Pacific coast. When the Army Corps of Engineers built the Route 1 Coastal Highway that spans from Mexico to Canada, they looked at the Lost Coast, saw the rugged terrain of the King Mountain range falling into the Pacific, and said “No thanks. At 1 am at ‘Shanty’, one of the city’s many dive bars you get the sense that the people of Eureka may indeed be from some retro-future grunge utopia. Whether you are Chris Durant, the co-founder of (the now defunct) Savage Henry Magazine and current owner of The Savage Henry Comedy Club, a heckler from the show that tagged along to say sorry (but mostly to stay out longer with her girlfriends), or one of the guys at a table and world of their own playing Magic the Gathering; everyone’s  found their place. They play their parts perfectly. They know each other. They are genuinely stoked you’re there with them.</p><p>Lately comedy has sort of felt like the road to Eureka for me. At the end of this winding road I don’t think the dense Redwoods open to a bustling metropolis like Los Angeles. I’m increasingly OK with this.</p><p>Most of my peers from when I started doing standup in 2018 have moved or are moving south to LA. Sometimes I think “I’m too old for that” but the reality is I’m too old to want that. There are two main reasons for this.</p><p>The first is that the road to LA sucks. It’s a straight shot on the 5 through scorched earth. It’s hot. There will be bumper to bumper traffic. The air unfit to breathe and when you finally get to LA the you’re journey has just begun.</p><p>The second is that so much of what LA has to offer is stuff a younger me wanted: nightlife, excitement, fame.
