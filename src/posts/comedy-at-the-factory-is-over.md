friends!</figcaption></figure></div><h4>Longer Version</h4><p>In March 2022 I started running standup comedy shows at The Factory Bar in Richmond CA. The first show sold out. The second show sold out. Then for the next three years pretty much every show sold out. In 2024 the show won “Best Comedy Night” in the East Bay Express reader poll. In 2025, we won second place.</p><p>Forget all that. I am convinced that on any given Tuesday or Wednesday night Comedy At The Factory was the best comedy show in the Bay Area. On some nights I really felt like it was the best show in the world, period. Where else were you going to see a show with someone with a Netflix or HBO special in a 42 person room? It was magic.</p><p>And now, it’s time for me to step down as producer. I’m turning over the reins to my friend <a href="https://www.instagram.com/jd__arandia/">JD Arandia</a>, a tremendously talented comedian that is from Richmond, CA. I’m really excited to see where JD takes things. I’m confident the show is in good hands and that Factory Bar will continue to be an incredible venue for stand up comedy for years to come. Again, the new link is: <a href="https://factorybarcomedy.eventbrite.com/">https://factorybarcomedy.eventbrite.com/</a> Bookmark it. Keep coming to the show. Keep supporting comedy in Richmond, The Factory Bar in general, and specifically COMEDY AT THE FACTORY!</p><h4>In addition to all that:</h4><p>I want to say thank you to:</p><ol><li><p>Tom, Michele, Marcus and the entire staff at The Factory Bar. You all created an incredibly cool place that is a hub for actual real community in Richmond. I’ve produced hundreds of shows in dozens of venues since I started performing standup in 2018 and The Factory Bar is by far the most supportive venue and ownership I have ever worked with. You probably didn’t know it when you painted the walls blue and decorated the walls like a Richmond-themed Cracker Barrel but your creation the Polka Dot Room was and is perfect for stand up comedy. My job was easy, all I had to do was book comedians that didn’t suck.</p></li><li><p>Thank you to all the comedians that came to do the show! Thank you to Bay Area comics for being unusually talented and professional. Thank you to the headliners that can fill theaters on their own for taking a gamble on a show that I described in an Instagram message as “OK it’s a bar show but like… it’s a good bar show?”. Everyone who ever did this show rules.</p></li><li><p>Thank you the audience and especially the show’s regulars! Most recurring shows have a different audience every single night. A really good show will sometimes have a handful of hardcore fans that come a few times a year. Most nights at The Factory Bar it felt like two-thirds of the audience were at the last show and the one before that. That’s incredible. You all are amazing. Richmond has more the highest per capita extremely-dedicated-comedy-fan rate in the nation. Someone tell the news.</p></li></ol><p>I’m planning another post soon with the juicy details about why I decided to step down but you’ll need to subscribe to get get notified. If you’re new here poke around this substack ‘The Leftovers’ to see what a ‘normal’ post has been in the past. Usually comedic essays at least one of which I read as my set at the comedy bar (“That time I cried watching Fast and The Furious”).</p><p>I’m hoping to write more now that I’m not producing!</p><div
